C R E D V I S T A

Product Requirements Document

What CredVista must deliver, for whom, and how success is measured

PRD  •  Version 1.0  •  19 October 2026

1. Executive summary

CredVista is a credit-risk and loan-portfolio analytics application for lending operations, risk analysts and portfolio managers. It consolidates borrower, loan and repayment data into a clear, explainable view of portfolio exposure and repayment performance. The first release focuses on analytics and rule-based segmentation—not automated credit approval or a trained default-prediction model.

2. Problem statement

Lending teams need to understand outstanding exposure, repayment delays, defaults and differences between borrower segments. When data is scattered across files or tables, it is difficult to compare performance consistently, investigate risk drivers and report trends. CredVista provides a shared, traceable view with clearly defined metrics and drill-downs.

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals

Provide a single, easy-to-navigate view of loan portfolio health.

Help analysts segment borrowers using transparent, configurable business rules.

Enable comparisons by credit score, DTI, risk band and loan origination cohort.

Make every headline metric traceable to a definition, reporting period and source records.

Export concise, repeatable portfolio reports.

Non-goals for MVP

Automatically approve or reject loan applications.

Claim to predict an individual's probability of default using ML.

Replace a lender's loan-origination system, servicing platform or core banking system.

Make legal, regulatory or credit-policy decisions on behalf of a lender.

Require complex workflow automation or a large enterprise integration before core analytics work.

6. Metric definitions (MVP)

Metric

Proposed definition

Important rule

Outstanding principal

Sum of remaining principal balance for loans in scope

Exclude closed/settled loans unless historical exposure is requested

Overdue amount

Sum of unpaid amounts past due as of the reporting date

Do not equate overdue amount with total outstanding principal

Default rate

Defaulted loans / loans in the defined eligible population

Document default definition, denominator and observation window

Repayment rate

Amount paid / amount due for the selected due population and period

State whether partial payments and fees are included

DTI

Monthly debt obligations / gross monthly income × 100

Use a consistent income frequency and debt-obligation definition

Risk-band share

Borrowers or exposure in a risk band / corresponding portfolio total

Label whether share is by borrower count or monetary exposure

Vintage default rate

Defaults within a defined origination cohort by a specified loan age / eligible cohort population

Compare like-for-like loan ages; mark immature cohorts as not yet observable

7. Business rules and risk segmentation

Risk thresholds must be configuration, not scattered hard-coded values. Initial Low/Medium/High thresholds are placeholders until the project dataset and business policy are reviewed. The rules engine should produce: assigned band, contributing factors, rule version, evaluation date and any missing-input warning. A missing credit score or income must not silently be treated as low risk.

Support rule precedence and deterministic outcomes for identical inputs.

Separate business risk bands from repayment status such as Active, Overdue, Defaulted or Closed.

Do not mix borrower-level and loan-level metrics without explicitly defining the aggregation.

Allow a borrower to have multiple loans while preserving unique borrower and loan identifiers.

Treat sensitive or protected attributes according to applicable policy; do not introduce them as risk features by default.

9. Success metrics

An analyst can locate a borrower or loan using search and filters without scanning the full dataset.

Every headline KPI can be reconciled to a documented query and sample records.

Risk classifications are reproducible and include an explanation.

Vintage comparisons do not compare immature cohorts as if they had equal observation time.

Exports reproduce the selected period and filters.

Usability review confirms that the dashboard is understandable without crowding or excessive chart density.

10. Dependencies, assumptions and risks

Required source data includes stable borrower/loan identifiers, origination dates, repayment schedule/payment records and current or historical balances.

Default definition, reporting timezone, DTI inputs and treatment of partial payments must be agreed before final KPI implementation.

Sample dashboard numbers are illustrative design content and must not be treated as real portfolio statistics.

Historical vintage analysis requires dated observations or repayment events; a current snapshot alone may not reconstruct past performance.

Data quality, incomplete history and inconsistent definitions can distort portfolio comparisons.

11. MVP release criteria

All Must requirements are implemented and verified against a representative dataset.

Metric formulas and denominators are documented and reviewed.

Risk rules are configurable, versioned and covered by tests.

Borrower/loan drill-down, filters and export behave consistently.

Security and data-quality checks pass; no secrets or unnecessary personal data are exposed.

UI review confirms a premium, uncluttered and responsive experience.
